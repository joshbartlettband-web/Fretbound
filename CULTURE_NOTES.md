# Cultural review (v1.67, 2026-10-04)

I went through every venue, caller, rule, call name, line of dialogue and liner note, and checked the facts behind them.

## Changed
- **Mister Midnight (Hollow Pine Juke).** His old look was a near-black-skinned man with glowing white eyes and a wide white grin, a Delta bluesman cast as the devil's go-between. That combination reads as a minstrel caricature, and it ties Black musicians to the devil, which is how the blues was smeared in its own time. He is now a moon spirit: a glowing pale moon for a head, top hat, purple suit, white gloves, resonator guitar. New pose set, portrait and code fallback; the old painted body is removed.
- **Liner fixes.** Slack key is spelled kī hōʻalu, and Honolulu, Hawaiʻi. Emperor is off the black metal Listen line, since band members were convicted of church arson and murder. Immortal and Enslaved stay.
- **Keep reading pages** (2 or 3 short pages, BACK/NEXT, no scrolling) for 18 venues, on the Liner Notes tab and on each caller's roster card. They cover who the music belongs to and where it came from, and say plainly what in the game is fantasy:
  - Bakersfield: the Dust Bowl migrants.
  - Surf: Dick Dale's Lebanese family and Misirlou.
  - Delta: the African American roots, and the Tommy and Robert Johnson crossroads myth.
  - Desert blues: Kel Tamasheq exile, and the Festival au Désert going into exile in 2012.
  - Sebene: Lingala, and La Sape.
  - Azmari: azmaris, the coffee ceremony, and how the game simplifies the qenet.
  - Bossa: samba's Afro-Brazilian roots.
  - Tango: candombe and the Afro-Argentine roots.
  - Chicha: Andean migrants in Lima.
  - Jazz manouche: the Sinti and the Porajmos.
  - Flamenco: the Gitano, and what duende means.
  - Black metal: the church burnings, including Fantoft in Bergen.
  - Kitsune: Inari shrines and etiquette.
  - Varanasi: the ghats and the Ganga aarti.
  - Bali: gamelan in temple life, and the all-women ensemble of Peliatan.
  - Slack key: the vaqueros, and the honu as ʻaumakua.
  - Alice Springs and Uluṟu: Mparntwe, Arrernte and Aṉangu.
  - Waitomo: Tāne Tinorau, the 1904 seizure and the 1990 return.

## Checked and kept
- **The Kitsune at a neon shrine.** Foxes really are Inari's messengers, so this is respectful. The liner page says it's fantasy and gives shrine etiquette.
- **Uluṟu in the Red Centre window.** It's a distant sunset view only, which matches what Aṉangu ask of photographers.
- **The ghats.** The Tiger plays at dusk, beside the aarti, not over it.
- **The four qenet.** The notes are correct, simplified to four.

## Judgement calls for Josh (not changed)
- **The Metronome Monk.** He wears saffron and crimson Buddhist robes. Robes are religious dress, and a few people may find a monk as a guitar hero flippant. I think it reads as affectionate, but it's your call.
- **The Dune Looper.** A faceless, hooded sand spirit at a Tuareg festival. Tuareg men's indigo veils are cultural dress, so a faceless figure could read as a comment on that. It's clearly a spirit made of sand, so I left it.
- **The Frost Cathedral.** The name sits next to a scene that burned churches. The liner now says so, and the hall is plainly a fantasy ice hall.
- **The Emperor at McMurdo Station.** He's a penguin. No issue; noted for completeness.
