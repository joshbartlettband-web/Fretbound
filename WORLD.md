# The world of Fretbound (draft, 2026-10-16)

A starting point to build on with Josh, not a finished canon. Every new character, event and piece of art should pass the tests below.

## The feel in one line
A casually magical version of our world. More 100 gecs than Harry Potter: chaotic, a bit lazy about its magic, and everybody still has time to stop and listen when music happens nearby.

## Reference: 100 gecs and their wizards (Josh's homework, 2026-10-16)
The touchstone for the tone. 100 gecs wear wizard robes and hats, first in the "mememe" video, and in "Doritos & Fritos" (directed by Chris Maggio) they fly over the city in those robes while the local TV news covers it like ordinary weather. "800db cloud" has them peeking out from behind buildings and trees around LA. What to take from it:
- **Deadpan magic.** Flying wizards are a local news item, not an event. Nobody is amazed for long.
- **Cheap costume, real joy.** The robes look homemade. Magic here is a bit of a bedsheet-and-glitter costume, worn with total commitment.
- **Chaos with a big heart.** Loud, silly, maximalist, but never mean. People are having fun.
- **The everyday city is the stage.** Strip malls, suburbs, parking lots, the sky over them.
Possible uses: a road event where the local news is covering your band's van as weather; a caller or player who is plainly a guy in a homemade wizard robe; flyers and posters with that DIY look.

## How the magic works (barely)
- Magic is ordinary and slightly underwhelming. It is in the background, like weather or a neighbour's wifi. Nobody studies it; people just put up with it.
- It does small, specific, faintly useless things. A pie that remembers your order. A fuzz pedal that still rains a little indoors. A motel ice machine that is haunted, friendly, and makes ghost-shaped cubes.
- It never solves the problem. The flat tire is still flat. Magic adds a texture or a joke, never a shortcut.
- Modern life is all still here: microwaves, gas stations, bad motel coffee, phones with cracked screens. The magic sits next to it, not instead of it.

## The test for any magical thing
Ask: is it soft, a bit worn, and doing its best? If it is sleek, powerful or epic, it is wrong for this world.
- Yes: a scruffy unicorn who used to host a public-television counting show in the 90s and now gives rides, mostly for the conversation.
- No: a gleaming self-driving super-unicorn.

## Who is who
- **Players are travelling musicians from a storybook guild.** Think trade guild more than wizard school: you earn your name by playing, and the guild is loose, a bit disorganised, and mostly a group chat. Each player is a guild archetype (the Bard, the Hermit, the Blacksmith, the Cartographer...). They live in the modern world but look like they stepped out of a storybook that has been left in a tour van for a while.
- **Callers are the spirits of a music's place.** Each one is what a style of music would be if it walked around: the Reverb Siren is surf, Mister Midnight is the crossroads, the Duende is flamenco's fire. They are characters, never caricatures of real people or cultures.
- **Real people appear only in the liner notes.** The real history (the musicians, the records) stays true and respectful in the liner notes. The fantasy never stands in for a real person.
- **The band and the crew are guild members too**, just with day jobs: a drummer who keeps time for a living, a sound engineer who believes in reverb the way some people believe in horoscopes.

## Recurring locals (seeds)
- **Gerald**, a soft, scruffy unicorn in a knitted scarf. Host of *Counting with Gerald* on public television, 1994 to 1999. Everybody remembers the counting song; nobody remembers the season about feelings, which he is quietly proud of. Gives rides anywhere. Conversation required. (First appears in the road event "A unicorn carpool", v1.91.)
- **The pawn shop owner** who knows the history of every haunted pedal on the wall.
- **The superfan** who drives six hours in a homemade shirt with your face on it. Their cat makes requests.

## The van
The van is a little bit alive (Josh, 2026-10-16). Not a talking car: it honks when it has an opinion, sulks in the cold, and seems faintly embarrassed when it gets a flat. It has been on tour longer than any of you. In the game it speaks only in honks (voice `van`), for example in the flat tire event.

## Characters to bring into line
- **The Journeyman** reads as a real session player from today (cardigan, flat cap). Proposed: a travelling guild journeyman, with a guild sash and badge, a road cloak, and picks and capos worn like tools on a belt.
- **The Busker** reads as modern street style (denim jacket with pins). Proposed: a street minstrel, keeping the cap and the pins but with storybook layers.
- **The Herald** honours the real Black founders of rock; her liner notes carry that history. Only a light touch if Josh wants one (for example a herald's sash). Her purpose stays as it is.

## Questions to explore together
1. What is the guild called, and what does joining it look like? A card? A patch? A song you have to play?
2. Why is everyone on tour? Is there a reason the world needs travelling musicians (magic runs better with live music nearby?), or is it just what people do?
3. What do ordinary people do with magic at work? Who else has a former-celebrity animal on staff?
4. The van is a little bit alive. What else does it do? Does it have a name, favourite roads, a grudge against a particular gas station?
5. What happens to callers between shows? Do they have day jobs too?
6. Where do the haunted pedals come from?
